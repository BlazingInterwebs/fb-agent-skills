# Worked example: one real situation, several possible jobs

All material below is synthetic and hypothetical, not a client result.

## Source brief

A small business tested an eleven-field onboarding form. Three testers said
they couldn't tell which fields were required. The team marked required fields
and plans a second test. No conversion outcome has been measured. Assume the
form screenshot is owned and contains no identifying information.

## Feed post

> Three people hit the same problem in our onboarding test: they couldn't tell
> which fields they had to fill in.
>
> We had eleven fields and no clear distinction. We've marked the required ones
> and are testing again.
>
> The lesson so far isn't that shorter forms convert better. It's that our form
> asked people to guess.
>
> Where does your process make someone guess?

The opening is concrete, the scope stays honest, and the question relates to
the lesson. A question is optional, not a required performance tactic.

## Repurposing opportunities

| Surface/format | Independent reader value | Required evidence/asset |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public Page text | Recognize an avoidable usability problem | Supplied observation; no conversion claim |
| Video demonstration | See the ambiguity and proposed fix | Owned redacted screenshot/recording |
| Image sequence | Compare the form states | Owned before/after images; current composer check |
| Story | Understand one small progress update | Actual screenshot; conditional interactive tools |
| Relevant Group | Discuss similar friction without pitching | Local rules and member role |

These aren't mandatory derivatives. Choose what the brief and capacity support.

## Growth plan and experiment

Editorial promise: practical small process improvements, with honest test results.
Possible series: 'Where our process made people guess'. Each installment needs
a new supported situation and a complete useful takeaway, not repeated bait.

Hypothesis: showing a concrete before/after helps viewers understand the change.
Test several comparable process demonstrations, using actual available metrics
at matched publication age. Retention can answer an attention question only if
supplied. Shares or attributed follows can inform other questions; unknown
metrics aren't invented. Evaluate volume, rates where defined, and production
effort. A single outcome doesn't establish a universal opening formula.
