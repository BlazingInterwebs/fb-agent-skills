# Voice review, not a universal style filter

Compare cadence, directness, humor, preferred terminology, perspective, level
of detail, and relationship with the audience. Prefer several permissioned
examples over a personality inferred from one message. Separate stable voice
preferences from a single piece's purpose. A formal Page announcement and an
informal personal update may legitimately sound different.

Mark passages as keep / revise / clarify with an actual reason. Useful
questions: would this person say this? Does it preserve their point? Is the
claim grounded? Does the sentence require context a stranger lacks? Is a
question genuinely worth answering? Is a flourish natural or imposed?

No word is universally banned. A user-specific avoid/prefer list is optional
context, not a reason to modify installed instructions. Emoji, curly quotes,
em dashes and Unicode format characters are not proof of AI authorship.
Some invisible characters are essential to languages or emoji sequences.
The helper flags a small set for inspection; it never rewrites or deletes them.

Examples are hypothetical:

- 'We've unlocked seamless customer journeys' may need revision if the user
  normally names concrete changes, but 'customer journey' may be their real term.
- 'Three people couldn't find the required fields' should remain a measured
  observation, not become 'Everyone was abandoning our form'.
- 'Thoughts?' can be a legitimate brief question in an established conversation;
  it isn't automatically spam. Review the surrounding purpose and context.

Fidelity outranks a cleaner-looking sentence. Never invent grammatical errors,
vulnerability, first-person experience, dates, quotes, or numbers for authenticity.
