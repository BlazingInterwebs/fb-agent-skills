# Video modes

## Short demonstration or story

Build around one complete idea. The opening should show or state the concrete
situation quickly, with enough orientation for an unfamiliar viewer. Develop
the mechanism, decision, or change; show the useful result. A loop is optional,
not a reason to conceal the ending. Choose duration based on the idea and
actual speaking/demonstration time, not an imported platform limit.

Beat sheet columns: estimated time, spoken line, visual/action, overlay,
purpose, asset status. Label timing as estimated until a recording exists.
Cover and caption should support the same honest promise without redundant
copy. Do not assume music in a reference is licensed for the user's reuse.

## Longer video

Use an opening promise and clear scope. Organize explanation around what the
viewer needs to understand next, not a list of disconnected tips. Examples,
demonstrations, questions and consequences sustain attention by adding meaning.
Resolve the initial promise and distinguish observed facts from opinion. Do
not copy YouTube chapter rules or thumbnail click-through assumptions into
Facebook; use those mechanics only where confirmed relevant.

## Live preparation

Prepare purpose, run-of-show, demonstrations, audience questions, optional
moderator notes, and a fallback if no questions arrive. Flag identifying
information that could appear on-screen. A moderation recommendation is not
authorization to delete/ban. Identify owned-source clip ideas for later, and
remind the user to preserve authorized originals where the current Live
storage policy matters. This mode does not operate streaming software.

## Review questions

Does the first frame/line accurately orient the viewer? Is the payoff complete?
What would a person who missed the original source misunderstand? Is each
visual feasible with available assets? Are timestamps observed or estimated?
Do claims survive fact and permission checks? For a series, what genuinely
useful future installment justifies returning?
