# Audit procedure

1. Define the user question and objective. Growth, helpful conversation,
   traffic, and qualified business outcomes can favor different content.
2. Inventory evidence and permissions. Record date/window, timezone, surface,
   format, publication age, export semantics, and known promotion.
3. Preserve raw inputs; normalize to a documented schema. Unknowns remain
   unknown. Do not rename views as reach or sum post reach as unique account reach.
4. Segment comparable data. Consider evolving account size, events, paid boosts,
   metric changes, and post age. Show counts alongside rates with valid
   denominators. A high small-sample rate can coexist with low useful volume.
5. Compare strong, typical and weak pieces. Record mechanisms as hypotheses
   unless a suitable experiment supports stronger claims. Look for counterexamples.
6. Prioritize specific changes by the user's objective and production capacity.
   State evidence strength, alternative explanations, and uncertainty.
7. Create experiment cards: hypothesis, feasible change, primary metric,
   observation window/publication age, confounders, decision criterion, next action.
8. Pass findings and conditions to planning without turning a temporary
   observation into a permanent rule or editing installed methodology.

## Helpful output

Coverage: e.g. 18 owner-supplied Page posts, public organic, two formats, same
seven-day snapshots; no follower attribution or retention. Findings attach
post IDs and definitions. Recommendations explain what the data can and cannot
support. No global score substitutes for measured results.

For comment quality, distinguish genuine participants/questions from creator
replies, spam and keyword responses where records permit. Do not claim hidden
private shares or message content. For conversions, specify attribution model
and window; correlation alone does not establish causality.
